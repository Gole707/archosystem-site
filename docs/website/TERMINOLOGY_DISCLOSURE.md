# ArchoSystem Website Rebuild — Terminology and Disclosure Rules

## 1. Governing principle

Public marketing should communicate **what the system enables, how it is configured, and why that matters** without turning one commercial material construction method into the product identity or publishing the complete construction recipe.

Patent, engineering, investor, and marketing language serve different purposes:

- **Patent language** protects the broad architecture and variations.
- **Engineering language** is precise enough to design, fabricate, test, and inspect.
- **Investor language** explains defensibility, scale, economics, and risk removal.
- **Public marketing language** explains benefits, choices, evidence, and applications.

Marketing is controlled simplification. It must not be false, absolute, or needlessly revealing.

## 3. Broad technical and patent-style descriptions

These phrases may be used in controlled technical or legal documents where appropriate:

- flexible, pressurizable spaced-wall structural assembly;
- opposing flexible barriers maintained at a predetermined separation by internal tensile spacing elements;
- three-dimensional textile-reinforced membrane assembly;
- internally restrained inflatable shell;
- fluid-fillable structural wall cavity;
- selectively fillable structural cavity configured to receive gas, liquid, granular, cementitious, composite, or combined media;
- content-specific inner containment barrier;
- environment-specific outer protective layer;
- compartmentalized structural wall with isolated zones;
- full-height or factory-joined wall assembly;
- replaceable external protective bands or jackets;
- integrated monitoring and lifecycle-record system.

Exact claim language is controlled by patent counsel and is not copied directly into public pages without review.

---

## 4. Terms that are not the public product identity

The following may describe an embodiment internally, but should not lead public marketing:

- drop-stitch;
- inflatable pool wall;
- swimming-pool technology;
- oobleck;
- cornstarch mixture;
- plain water wall;
- fabric tank;
- air mattress;
- bladder tank, unless used in a competitor comparison;
- four-foot ring system as a universal architecture;
- Kevlar tank;
- concrete balloon;
- UHPC tank as the only ArchoTank definition.

Reasons:

- they narrow the invention;
- they anchor the product to a commodity comparison;
- they disclose mechanism before value;
- they may create inaccurate performance expectations;
- they do not represent the full configuration platform.

## 5. Public layer description

A public functional diagram may show:

1. stored contents;
2. content-specific inner barrier;
3. structural wall system;
4. selected structural or activation medium;
5. replaceable exterior protection;
6. base, anchoring, and process interfaces;
7. monitoring and lifecycle record.

A public diagram should not show, label, dimension, or explain:

- exact internal tensile-member pattern;
- yarn count or density;
- seam geometry;
- weld overlap or adhesive system;
- exact pressure zones;
- proprietary formulation chemistry;
- exact penetration and repair details;
- confidential attachment rails or connection geometry;
- bills of material;
- supplier identity without approval;
- weapon-threat construction details.

## 7. Acceptable and unacceptable examples

### Product definition

**Acceptable public:**

> ArchoTank is a configurable structural containment system designed around transportable deployment, application-specific containment, selectable protection, and lifecycle validation.

**Too narrow:**

> ArchoTank is a drop-stitch water tank.

**Unsupported:**

> ArchoTank stores any liquid anywhere with no maintenance.

### Capacity

**Acceptable public:**

> Capacity is configured through engineered diameter and wall height. Representative geometries range from field-scale systems to infrastructure-scale reservoirs, with final usable capacity established by the project design.

**Unsupported:**

> One system replaces every tank from 10,000 to one million gallons.

### Climate

**Acceptable public:**

> Climate-specific formulations and exterior packages are being developed for freezing, warm, and high-heat environments.

**Too revealing:**

> We add a specific percentage of named chemical X, thickener Y, and additive Z to local water.

**Unsupported:**

> Works in every climate.

### Damage tolerance

**Acceptable public before testing:**

> The validation program includes compartmentation, controlled damage, leak detection, isolation, field repair, and redeployment.

**Unsupported:**

> Knife-proof, bulletproof, self-sealing, blast-proof, or indestructible.

### Permanent and hardened configurations

**Acceptable public:**

> Permanent cementitious and hardened configurations are part of the development program.

**Unsupported:**

> UHPC makes the tank blast-proof.

## 8. Market language

Public pages may identify selected sectors and common infrastructure problems.

Private investor pages may show:

- sector size;
- relevant containment share;
- buyer classes;
- geographic priorities;
- unit-based capture scenarios;
- revenue stack;
- barriers and procurement routes;
- confidence and evidence.

Do not publish a large global market total and imply that ArchoTank can capture a percentage without defining the relevant segment, technical fit, buying unit, and reachable buyer population.

---

## 9. Image disclosure rules

Public concept images must avoid exposing:

- cross-sections with exact internal architecture;
- detailed seams and connection layouts;
- proprietary filling or assembly sequences;
- weapon-hardening layer counts;
- detailed repair and isolation components.

Images must not imply:

- an existing customer deployment;
- military or agency endorsement;
- certification;
- proven ballistic or blast resistance;
- operating scale not identified as conceptual.

Use a concise concept-image label where the surrounding page could reasonably be read as a completed installation.

---

## 10. Final language review

Before release, every page receives four checks:

1. **Patent/disclosure:** Does the copy narrow the system or reveal unnecessary mechanism?
2. **Claims:** Is every material claim in the claims ledger with evidence and allowed wording?
3. **Customer clarity:** Can a non-specialist understand the value and next action?
4. **Consistency:** Are product names, configuration terms, and development-status words used the same way across the site?
