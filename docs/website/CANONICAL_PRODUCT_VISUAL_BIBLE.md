# ArchoTank Canonical Product Visual Bible v1

**Status:** Gate 2 baseline  
**Purpose:** Lock one recognizable product family before cinematic image generation, wireframe polish, or production layout

---

## 1. Governing visual decision

ArchoTank must look like credible near-future infrastructure, not an inflatable pool, fantasy machine, generic steel tank, or unrelated collection of AI concepts.

Every public and private image will use the same core product silhouette and detail language. Service class, exterior package, accessories, site integration, and scale may change; the underlying family identity may not.

Existing ArchoTank concept images remain useful references for modular staging, customer skins, and support equipment, but they are not the final canonical product because they mix earlier UHPC/POD language, inconsistent tank proportions, oversized identity graphics, and different wall and roof treatments.

---

## 2. Canonical silhouette

### Core form

- broad cylindrical ground-storage form;
- continuous full-height wall appearance;
- no universal four-foot ring segmentation;
- no inflated-tube or air-mattress appearance;
- no exaggerated bulging;
- slight structural tension is acceptable, but the wall must read as stable infrastructure;
- circular geometry is the public default; gently faceted geometry may appear only when intentional and consistent.

### Proportion family

The default visual family uses a diameter-to-wall-height ratio between approximately **2.5:1 and 5:1**.

The primary public hero reference proportion is approximately:

- **40 ft diameter**;
- **12 ft wall height**;
- nominal geometric capacity approximately 112,800 gallons before freeboard and operating allowances.

This dimension is a visual and capacity reference, not a certified product configuration.

The same family may be shown as:

- field: 15–25 ft diameter, 4–10 ft wall height;
- utility: 30–60 ft diameter, 8–20 ft wall height;
- reservoir: 60–140+ ft diameter, engineered height;
- compact-footprint: greater wall height with correspondingly stronger access, stability, and structural expression.

---

## 3. Wall language

### Surface

- matte graphite or deep-charcoal base finish;
- restrained fine textile/composite microtexture;
- visually continuous from base to roof interface;
- subtle broad vertical or circumferential rhythm may suggest engineered fabrication without showing exact seams;
- no random stitched lines, exposed fibers, unsupported wrinkles, or decorative panelization;
- no glossy vinyl-toy appearance.

### Edge conditions

- clean dark base collar;
- narrow top perimeter cap or compression edge;
- controlled shadow line where replaceable exterior packages meet the core;
- realistic local reinforcement around process interfaces without showing proprietary construction.

### Wall thickness

The wall must appear materially substantial and load-bearing. Camera views may show a believable top or opening edge, but exact wall thickness is not labeled or dimensioned publicly.

---

## 4. Roof family

### Public default

A low-profile shallow dome or tensioned roof with a clean perimeter interface.

Characteristics:

- low rise;
- no inflated bubble appearance;
- no oversized roof ribs;
- one or two realistic vents where required;
- compatible with compact transport and field installation in rapid/reusable scenes;
- visually related to the permanent roof family.

### Permanent option

A shallow metal/composite dome or low conical roof integrated with permanent access, vents, overflow, and civil site work.

### Process/open-top option

May be used on selected wastewater, process, or temporary-basin pages, but is not the main ArchoTank identity.

---

## 5. Base family

### Rapid/reusable

- low dark base collar or skirt;
- prepared compacted pad, ground-protection system, or low modular foundation;
- visible restraint only where operationally coherent;
- nearby deployment skid, hoses, pumps, or control equipment may establish temporary use;
- no oversized permanent concrete foundation.

### Semi-permanent

- engineered low plinth or ring interface;
- more developed anchoring and process piping;
- modular service platform;
- durable exterior package.

### Permanent

- integrated civil ring foundation or slab;
- drainage and access clearly resolved;
- fixed process connections;
- permanent ladder, stair, platform, or roof access;
- architectural site integration.

---

## 6. Service and accessory zone

Process interfaces are grouped into one disciplined service zone instead of scattered randomly around the tank.

The canonical service zone may include:

- inlet and outlet connections;
- drain and sample point;
- protected valve grouping;
- level or pressure instrumentation;
- small control or sensor enclosure;
- vertical ladder or stair access;
- compact inspection platform where required;
- pipe supports and protective guards.

Rules:

- piping must connect to a believable destination;
- valves, vents, ladders, and platforms must be correctly scaled;
- no decorative pipes;
- no impossible penetrations;
- fittings are protected in hardened variants but not hidden behind fantasy armor.

---

## 7. Access family

### Low wall height

- removable ladder or compact stair;
- no unnecessary full-height cage;
- small top landing only where needed.

### Medium and tall wall height

- permanent or modular stair/ladder family;
- safety cage or fall-protection logic where height warrants it;
- service platform concentrated near the access and process zone;
- no default full-circumference roof railing unless the depicted operation requires it.

---

## 8. Branding and identity

### ArchoSystem standard

- small ArchoTank wordmark near the service zone;
- restrained ArchoSystem mark or serial identification;
- branding occupies a minor portion of the visible wall;
- no oversized billboard text on public hero assets.

### Customer identity skin

A replaceable exterior package may carry:

- customer colors and logo;
- municipal or agency identity;
- emergency high-visibility and reflective markings;
- low-visibility neutral;
- woodland, desert, snow, or marine environmental patterns;
- architectural screening or civic graphics;
- sponsor, donor, or humanitarian identity where appropriate;
- fleet number, serial number, and asset-status markings.

Customer or agency logos are used only with permission. Concept images use fictional or generic identity marks unless specifically authorized.

---

## 9. Exterior protection language

Exterior protection must read as an added, replaceable system rather than a different tank invention.

### Standard jacket

- UV, weather, and ordinary abrasion protection;
- visually close to the structural body;
- matte graphite default.

### Rugged lower band

- heavier lower 4 ft or 8 ft protection zone;
- distinct overlap and attachment language;
- protects high-contact areas from equipment, debris, and vandalism.

### Layered protection

- multiple overlapping exterior sections where needed;
- staggered vertical and horizontal joints;
- stand-off or energy-absorbing layer may be suggested without revealing exact construction;
- no ballistic, fragment, or blast claim unless the exact depicted system has been tested.

### Hardened visual variant

- restrained segmented exterior protection around vulnerable zones;
- protected fittings and service bay;
- heavier base and anchoring expression;
- no weapons, impacts, explosions, or “indestructible” imagery.

---

## 10. Service-class variants

### Rapid

- standard graphite wall;
- low-profile temporary roof;
- removable access;
- nearby deployment equipment;
- minimal permanent civil work;
- clean but clearly temporary site.

### Reusable

- slightly heavier base and exterior package;
- integrated fill/drain and monitoring connections;
- recovery and refurbishment equipment may be visible;
- still transportable and non-permanent.

### Semi-permanent

- durable exterior jacket;
- developed base and process connections;
- service platform and longer-duration site integration.

### Permanent

- architectural exterior finish;
- fixed roof and access;
- permanent civil base and piping;
- municipal or industrial site integration.

### Hardened

- layered exterior protection;
- protected service zone;
- stronger base/anchoring expression;
- no unsupported performance claim.

## 11. Color system

Use the existing ArchoSystem palette:

- black: `#050607`;
- graphite: `#0B0E12`;
- card dark: `#11161D`;
- elevated dark: `#161C24`;
- steel: `#A8B3C2`;
- white: `#F4F7FB`;
- muted gray: `#9AA4B2`;
- blue signal: `#3B82F6`;
- green signal: `#19C37D`;
- orange signal: `#D9822B`;
- cyan signal: `#45D9F0`.

Color is functional:

- blue: data, configuration, water, control;
- green: ready, validated, sustainable operation;
- orange: warning, deployment, heat, temporary operations;
- steel and graphite: core physical product.

Do not cover the canonical public asset in bright decorative colors unless demonstrating a customer identity package.

---

## 12. Camera and scene rules

### Public hero

- three-quarter wide view;
- camera near human eye level or moderately elevated;
- tank occupies 40–65% of frame;
- workers and service vehicle establish scale;
- early morning, late afternoon, or controlled overcast light;
- credible municipal/industrial site;
- open negative space for webpage copy;
- no cutaway.

### Product/catalog

- clean neutral or controlled industrial background;
- consistent camera height and lens;
- front three-quarter and side orthographic-like views;
- accessory and service details visible but not proprietary.

### Investor panorama

- cinematic aerial or elevated view;
- same recognizable tank family across sectors;
- no collage of unrelated product shapes;
- restrained interface overlays only where they add information.

## 13. Reference set to create before market scenes

Generate and lock only these first:

1. **REF-01 Rapid/reusable canonical tank** — graphite, 40 ft × 12 ft visual reference, low-profile roof, temporary base, service bay, removable access.
2. **REF-02 Permanent canonical tank** — same family, architectural finish, permanent roof, civil base, fixed platform and piping.
3. **REF-03 Hardened canonical tank** — same family, layered lower protection, protected service bay, no threat performance claim.
4. **REF-04 Identity-skin sheet** — standard, municipal, emergency, low-visibility, woodland, desert, snow, marine, and architectural variants on the same tank.
5. **REF-05 Scale family** — field, utility, and reservoir configurations with correct human and vehicle scale.

No public or investor market scene is generated until these references are approved.

---

## 14. Rejection criteria

Reject any image that:

- resembles a pool toy, air mattress, or ordinary bladder;
- changes the tank silhouette without an approved reason;
- shows ring segmentation as a universal design;
- uses a giant customer logo or unauthorized agency identity;
- shows impossible wall thickness, foundation, piping, ladder, or access;
- exposes exact internal construction;
- uses science-fiction lighting, glowing liquid, neon pipes, or holographic clutter;
- implies certification, deployment, customer use, ballistic, fragment, or blast performance that is not supported;
- cannot be cropped for desktop, tablet, and mobile without losing the product story;
- is merely attractive but does not communicate the assigned message.
