# ArchoSystem Website Rebuild — Visual System and Cinematic Shot List

## 1. Visual objective

The rebuilt experience should make ArchoSystem look like advanced infrastructure that could be photographed in the near future: ambitious and cinematic, but physically grounded enough to survive engineering, government, industrial, and investor scrutiny.

The visual system must communicate:

- infrastructure scale;
- compact transport and site deployment;
- one recognizable product family across configurations;
- municipal, emergency, industrial, remote, and hardened contexts;
- disciplined validation;
- the difference between the public product story and the private platform-scale opportunity.

The images do not replace evidence. They create context and belief while calculations, tests, sources, and status language provide credibility.

---

## 2. Existing brand DNA to retain

- matte graphite, black, and deep-charcoal foundation;
- restrained white and gray typography;
- monolithic geometry;
- premium industrial finish;
- defense-adjacent seriousness without tactical costume;
- controlled use of signal color rather than decorative gradients;
- minimal visual noise;
- clean, engineered sites;
- no generic SaaS glow;
- no fantasy machinery;
- no random futuristic pipes, holograms, or science-fiction interfaces.

### Visual modes

1. **Brand / Hero Mode** — cinematic first impression, quiet confidence, large scale, minimal labels.
2. **System / Control Mode** — lifecycle, configuration, monitoring, readiness, and validation; color is used only as a signal.
3. **Technical / Catalog Mode** — clean product families, accessories, diagrams, tables, and configuration comparisons.

---

## 3. Canonical ArchoTank visual bible

No large image set is generated until the canonical product is approved.

### 3.1 Common visual traits

- broad cylindrical or gently faceted ground-storage form;
- continuous full-height wall appearance unless a specific sectional configuration is intentionally shown;
- clean base interface;
- controlled roof family;
- realistic vents, access, and process connections;
- restrained ArchoSystem branding;
- believable wall thickness and edge details;
- no exposed proprietary internal architecture;
- no random seams, unsupported openings, or impossible piping;
- workers, vehicles, stairs, or equipment included where needed to establish scale.

### 3.2 Product variants

**Rapid / reusable**

- lighter external finish;
- visibly transportable support equipment;
- flexible but stable geometry;
- removable access and deployment accessories;
- staged or temporary site context.

**Semi-permanent**

- more developed base and process connections;
- durable exterior jacket;
- service platform and safety accessories;
- longer-duration site integration.

**Permanent**

- architectural finish;
- integrated civil base;
- permanent access and piping;
- municipal or industrial site integration.

**Hardened**

- replaceable heavy exterior zones and protected fittings;
- restrained mass and protection language;
- no visual claim of ballistic or blast rating unless tied to actual tested configuration.

### 3.3 Locked identity items

The visual bible must approve:

- canonical diameter-to-height proportions;
- wall surface texture;
- roof options;
- base profile;
- logo location and scale;
- fitting family;
- ladder and platform family;
- temporary versus permanent visual differences;
- exterior protection-band language;
- acceptable customer-logo, safety, camouflage, and architectural skin treatments.

## 6. Aesthetic and identity package imagery

The exterior skin catalog should look like industrial product customization, not costume design.

### Package families

- ArchoSystem standard graphite;
- municipal/customer color and logo;
- emergency high-visibility and reflective markings;
- low-visibility neutral;
- woodland, desert, snow, and marine environmental patterns;
- industrial safety bands;
- architectural screening and civic graphics;
- fleet numbering and asset identification;
- sponsor or humanitarian identity where appropriate.

### Rules

- Exterior identity skins remain visually separate from the structural wall.
- Logos and patterns do not imply customer use or endorsement without approval.
- Camouflage imagery is kept private or market-specific unless there is a clear public reason.
- Printed skins must not cover required labels, vents, sensors, access, or safety markings.
- Aesthetic treatments may be combined with UV, thermal, abrasion, or safety functions.

## 7. Image-generation workflow

### Stage 1 — Canonical product lock

Create a small reference set for:

- rapid/reusable;
- permanent;
- hardened.

Approve geometry, wall finish, roof, base, fittings, branding, and scale before creating market scenes.

### Stage 2 — Shot lock

For every image, approve:

- page and section;
- message;
- scene;
- camera angle;
- environment;
- product variant;
- scale references;
- public/private status;
- required label or disclaimer.

### Stage 3 — Limited generation

- Generate a controlled candidate set.
- Select one master.
- Allow one substantive correction pass.
- Lock the image.

Do not continue regenerating because a cloud, truck angle, worker location, or small crop might be marginally better.

### Stage 4 — Crop production

Create from the approved master:

- 16:9 desktop;
- 4:3 tablet;
- 4:5 mobile;
- 1.91:1 social preview where required.

Do not create a new product design for each breakpoint.

### Stage 5 — Image register

Record:

- image ID;
- source/master;
- concept label;
- approved use;
- public/private status;
- crop versions;
- alt text;
- claims or capacity values shown;
- final approval.

---

## 8. Prohibited visual patterns

- unrelated tank geometry between pages;
- impossible thin walls at large scale;
- exposed exact internal textile or connection architecture;
- unsupported cutaways;
- random military hardware or combat imagery;
- agency/customer logos without permission;
- flames, bullets, explosions, or impacts implying tested hardening;
- glowing liquid, neon piping, or magic materials;
- workers without realistic PPE or scale;
- tanks placed on visibly impossible foundations;
- generic stock tank photography presented as ArchoTank;
- excessive decorative interface overlays;
- crowded collages that prevent the user from understanding the product.

---

## 9. Acceptance criteria

An image is approved only if:

- it uses the canonical product form;
- the scale is believable;
- the application is operationally coherent;
- it communicates one primary idea;
- it does not reveal protected detail;
- it does not create an unsupported claim;
- it fits the existing ArchoSystem visual identity;
- required concept labeling is defined;
- desktop, tablet, and mobile crops remain usable;
- it is good enough to lock without further subjective iteration.
