# Investor Opportunity Atlas — Sector Card and Capture Template

## 1. Purpose

The private investor experience will use a global infrastructure view and consistent sector cards to show:

- the scale and structure of each market;
- the containment problem;
- where ArchoTank fits technically and commercially;
- who buys;
- how the company enters;
- what an initial system and installed base can generate;
- how much of the market is realistically reachable;
- what evidence supports the conclusion.

The experience must avoid turning every market into a huge undifferentiated TAM claim.

## 3. Three information levels

### Level 1 — Executive card

Purpose: allow an investor to understand the opportunity in seconds.

Fields:

- sector name;
- one-sentence problem;
- primary ArchoTank entry configuration;
- buyer class;
- representative capacity band;
- addressable opportunity range;
- strategic priority;
- evidence confidence;
- next validation milestone.

### Level 2 — Commercial drilldown

Purpose: explain how the business enters and earns revenue.

Fields:

- target geographies;
- buyer and operator types;
- use cases;
- current alternatives;
- procurement and sales route;
- expected buying unit;
- likely systems per buyer;
- initial system revenue components;
- accessories and aesthetic attach;
- recurring materials, software, service, and readiness;
- sales-cycle and adoption barriers;
- pilot design;
- partner strategy.

### Level 3 — Evidence and diligence

Purpose: make the thesis auditable.

Fields:

- source list;
- market definitions;
- included and excluded categories;
- installed asset base;
- replacement or expansion cycle;
- unit assumptions;
- price assumptions;
- calculations;
- confidence range;
- sensitivity analysis;
- technical suitability constraints;
- regulatory and certification requirements;
- unresolved questions.

## 6. TAM, SAM, and SOM calculation method

### 6.1 TAM

TAM is the total relevant spend or asset demand **after** isolating the sector’s applicable containment category.

Do not use the entire water, defense, agriculture, construction, or energy economy as ArchoTank TAM.

TAM inputs may include:

- relevant tank and containment spend;
- installed assets requiring replacement;
- new infrastructure demand;
- temporary-capacity and emergency inventory;
- accessories and lifecycle support where independently defined.

### 6.2 SAM

SAM removes applications outside the planned technical and commercial envelope:

- incompatible contents;
- unsupported pressure or temperature;
- unsuitable geometry;
- inaccessible certification path;
- geographies outside initial reach;
- buyer types without a practical route;
- applications where incumbents clearly remain better.

### 6.3 SOM

SOM is built from reachable buying units, not an arbitrary TAM percentage.

```text
reachable_buyers
× expected_systems_per_buyer
× average_initial_revenue_per_system
+
installed_assets
× average_annual_recurring_revenue_per_asset
=
scenario_revenue
```

Each sector includes:

- conservative scenario;
- base scenario;
- expansion scenario;
- assumptions and sensitivity;
- time horizon;
- evidence confidence.

## 7. Capture model fields

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| Reachable buyers | Named or countable organizations that fit the initial route |
| Systems per buyer | Initial order, staged reserve, site count, or fleet assumption |
| Average initial revenue | System, barrier, exterior, base, accessories, sensors, deployment |
| Accessory attach rate | Percentage and value of systems receiving add-ons |
| Recurring asset revenue | Formulation, replacement, ArchoOS, readiness, inspection, service |
| Replacement cycle | Evidence-based frequency or scenario range |
| Sales cycle | Research assumption with source and confidence |
| Win rate | Scenario input, never hidden |
| Production constraint | Maximum deliverable units under the scenario |
| Capital constraint | Funding or working-capital limitation |
| Evidence score | Confidence in buyer count, pricing, fit, and procurement route |

---

## 8. Confidence system

Every key number is tagged:

- **High confidence** — authoritative source or direct validated data;
- **Medium confidence** — multiple credible sources or strong proxy;
- **Low confidence** — preliminary inference requiring research;
- **Hypothesis** — strategic idea not yet modeled.

A visually large number may not be shown without its confidence and definition nearby.

## 9. Example qualitative sector card — Emergency Response

This example defines structure only. It is not a completed market model.

```text
SECTOR
Emergency response and temporary utility restoration

PROBLEM
Disasters can create immediate water, wastewater, fire-support, and process-storage demand in places where rigid infrastructure cannot arrive or be constructed quickly.

ENTRY CONFIGURATION
Rapid and reusable staged containment systems

PRIMARY BUYERS
Emergency-management agencies, utilities, disaster contractors, states, municipalities, military support organizations, industrial response firms

CAPACITY BAND
Project-dependent field and utility-scale systems

ARCHOTANK FIT
Compact staged package, configurable deployed geometry, climate package, replacement stock, accessories, recovery, refurbishment, and readiness tracking

REVENUE STACK
Staged systems + pumps/hoses/accessories + replacement barriers/protection + formulation replenishment + ArchoOS fleet readiness + annual inspection/training + post-deployment refurbishment

PRIORITY
Near-term pilot and strategic recurring-revenue market

EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE
Market need: high; ArchoTank product fit and unit economics: development-stage

NEXT PROOF
Buyer interviews, staged-package definition, storage-life test, timed deployment, recovery/repacking test, regional service model, pilot partner
```

## 10. Investor UI acceptance criteria

The Opportunity Atlas is ready when:

- every sector uses the same definitions and card structure;
- the executive level is understandable without opening diligence detail;
- TAM, SAM, and SOM do not mix incompatible definitions;
- the capture model is unit-based and auditable;
- sources, dates, assumptions, and confidence are visible;
- revenue includes installed-base layers rather than only new-system sales;
- sectors can be compared without implying identical sales cycles or margins;
- the global view reveals scale without becoming a cluttered decorative map;
- confidential economics and strategy remain behind authenticated access.
