## Section: The asset should fit the mission—not the truck

Conventional storage systems often force a choice between transportability and deployed scale. ArchoTank is being developed to separate the transported package from the infrastructure asset created at the site.

That changes the starting question from “What tank can we ship here?” to:

- How much capacity is required?
- What footprint and height can the site support?
- What will be stored?
- How long will the system remain in service?
- What climate, exposure, and protection level apply?
- What access, monitoring, and readiness support are required?

The system is then configured around those conditions.

## Section: One platform, configured by the project

### Capacity and geometry

### Stored contents

### Service class

### Climate and exposure

### Accessories and operations

### Monitoring and evidence

# 2. ArchoTank

## Metadata

**Title:** ArchoTank | Configurable Structural Containment  
**Description:** Explore the ArchoTank product architecture, service classes, containment barriers, climate packages, exterior protection, accessories, and validation program.

## Hero

**Eyebrow:** ArchoTank product family

# One containment platform. Multiple operating configurations.

ArchoTank is being developed as a configurable structural containment system rather than one fixed tank material, height, capacity, or service life.

The configuration begins with the project requirement and selects the geometry, inner containment barrier, structural medium, climate package, exterior protection, base and restraint, accessories, and monitoring package around that requirement.

## Section: Functional architecture

### Inner containment barrier

Separates the stored contents from the structural body and is selected around compatibility, sanitation, permeability, temperature, cleaning, and regulatory requirements.

### Structural wall system

Provides the engineered geometry and supports the selected service class while allowing compact transportation and site deployment.

### Structural or activation medium

Selected around speed, recoverability, mass, stiffness, permanence, damping, climate, and protection objectives.

### Exterior protection

Replaceable protection can be selected for UV, weather, abrasion, vandalism, puncture, thermal exposure, identity, environmental blending, and future tested hardening packages.

### Base and interfaces

Foundation, anchoring, restraint, vents, overflow, drainage, process connections, access, and instrumentation are part of the configured system.

### Monitoring and records

ArchoOS is planned to preserve identity, inspection, readiness, deployment, repair, replacement, and lifecycle evidence.

## Section: Service classes under development

### Rapid

Pneumatic structural configurations intended for the fastest deployment and recovery workflows.

### Reusable

Engineered-liquid and ballast configurations intended to add mass, damping, stability, and climate-specific performance while preserving recovery and redeployment.

### Semi-permanent

Longer-duration configurations combining durable barriers, exterior protection, anchoring, monitoring, and planned service procedures.

### Permanent

Cementitious configurations intended to convert the deployed structural system into a long-duration civil-infrastructure asset.

### Hardened

Future configurations combining engineered structural media and exterior protection for defined impact, fragment, or blast environments. Performance claims will be limited to stated, tested threats.

## Section: Built around independent choices

The same capacity may require different systems depending on what is stored and where it operates.

A raw-water system, wastewater system, fire-water system, industrial process system, and cold-weather emergency system should not be treated as identical simply because the gallon requirement is the same.

ArchoTank separates the primary decisions so that each can be qualified, tested, replaced, and improved without redefining the entire platform.

## Section: Full-height and factory-joined walls

The public product is not restricted to four-foot structural rings. Full-height or factory-joined wall configurations are the default development direction where they reduce field connections and improve continuity.

Commercial material widths and fabrication methods influence manufacturing layout, but they do not define the customer-facing capacity range.

## Section: Exterior protection and identity

The structural system can receive replaceable protection in localized bands, larger sections, or full-height jackets.

Potential packages include:

- standard weather and UV protection;
- heavy lower-wall abrasion protection;
- cut- and puncture-resistant layers;
- insulation and reflective skins;
- industrial and chemical exposure covers;
- customer logos and fleet identity;
- emergency high-visibility markings;
- camouflage and environmental finishes;
- architectural screening;
- future tested fragment and hardened packages.

## Section: Accessory ecosystem

The tank is only one part of an operating storage system.

ArchoSystem plans an accessory and fabrication catalog covering:

- ladders, stairs, platforms, handrails, and walkways;
- hatches and manways;
- vents, overflow, pressure/vacuum relief, drains, and sampling;
- inlet, outlet, mixing, and recirculation interfaces;
- brackets, supports, sensor rails, and equipment mounts;
- anchoring and restraint;
- pumps, blowers, dosing skids, hose reels, control boxes, and deployment trailers;
- insulation, roofs, weather packages, secondary containment, and ground protection.

# Start with the project. Configure the system around it.

A tank should not be selected by gallons alone. The useful configuration is the one that satisfies capacity, footprint, height, stored contents, service duration, environment, access, protection, operations, and evidence requirements together.

## Configuration path

### 1. Define the capacity

Set the target working volume, expected operating level, reserve requirement, and freeboard.

### 2. Define the site envelope

Set the maximum footprint, maximum height, access limitations, ground conditions, wind, flooding, settlement, utilities, and installation equipment.

### 3. Define the contents

Identify the material, concentration, temperature, sanitation, compatibility, permeability, cleaning, contamination, and regulatory requirements.

### 4. Define the service class

Choose the objective: rapid, reusable, semi-permanent, permanent, or hardened.

### 5. Define the climate and exposure

Identify freezing, heat, UV, marine, industrial, fire, abrasion, vandalism, puncture, visibility, camouflage, and architectural requirements.

### 6. Define operations

Select access, vents, overflow, drains, process connections, pumps, mixing, sampling, instruments, restraints, secondary containment, and deployment equipment.

### 7. Define readiness and records

Set inspection, staging, sensor, repair, replacement, training, recertification, and lifecycle-record requirements.

## Configuration outputs

A preliminary configuration may identify:

- diameter and wall height;
- nominal and working capacity;
- base hydrostatic pressure;
- service class;
- inner-barrier family;
- climate-package category;
- exterior-protection tier;

# 4. Capacity

## Hero

**Eyebrow:** Capacity and geometry

# Choose the volume. Compare the geometry.

The same storage requirement can be configured lower and wider for stability and reduced pressure, or taller and more compact where footprint is limited.

## Capacity formula

For a cylindrical tank:

`Nominal gallons = π × (diameter ÷ 2)² × height × 7.48052`

The result is nominal geometric volume. Final working capacity must account for freeboard, floor geometry, fittings, operating range, and project-specific limits.

## Representative capacity table

Use the approved table from the homepage.

## Section: Lower and wider

Advantages may include:

- lower base hydrostatic pressure;
- increased stability;
- easier access and inspection;
- less sensitivity to pressure loss;
- reduced wall demand;
- compatibility with height restrictions.

Tradeoffs include larger footprint, longer perimeter, more site preparation, and longer piping runs.

## Section: Taller and more compact

Advantages may include:

- reduced land use;
- shorter perimeter for a given volume;
- better fit on constrained sites.

Tradeoffs include greater hydrostatic demand, more demanding stability, wall, seam, anchoring, access, and failure-control requirements.

## Selector V1 introduction

Selector V1 is a transparent preliminary calculator. It does not produce a final design, quote, code review, or certified product selection.

Inputs:

- target capacity;
- maximum diameter or available footprint;
- maximum height;
- service class;
- stored-content category;
- climate;
- site access;
- protection requirement.

Outputs:

- recommended preliminary geometry;
- alternate low-and-wide and compact-footprint options;
- nominal and estimated working capacity;
- base hydrostatic pressure;
- preliminary configuration categories;
- required engineering-review notice.

## Selector disclaimer

Results are conceptual and calculated from user inputs and stated assumptions. Final dimensions, capacity, materials, barriers, pressure, anchoring, access, safety, and code compliance require project-specific engineering and validation.

# 6. Validation

## Metadata

**Title:** ArchoTank Validation Program | Calculation, Prototype and Test Evidence  
**Description:** Review the staged ArchoTank validation program covering materials, seams, geometry, pressure, retained liquid, climate, protection, damage, repair, recovery, and permanent configurations.

## Hero

**Eyebrow:** Validation program

# Every capability moves from calculation to evidence.

The purpose of the prototype program is not to prove that flexible containment exists. It is to determine the safe operating envelope of commercially available structural materials in ArchoTank geometry and confirm the calculations through staged physical tests.

## Current focus

- supplier and material data collection;
- geometry, capacity, hydrostatic, stability, and structural analysis;
- fabrication layouts and seam qualification;
- full-height prototype definition;
- instrumentation and pass/fail criteria;
- pneumatic and engineered-liquid test planning.

## Validation lanes

### Material and supplier qualification

Certified minimum properties, temperature range, pressure, seam, creep, puncture, UV, fold-cycle, fitting, and fabrication limits.

### Structural and geometric analysis

Hydrostatic pressure, circumferential demand, deformation, stability, anchoring, staged fill and drain, failure containment, and practical height-and-diameter envelopes.

### Fabrication and seams

Coupons, welded or bonded seams, curved geometry, fittings, valves, reinforcement, repeatability, inspection, and repair.

### Full-height pneumatic testing

Inflation, geometry, pressure retention, creep, deformation, lateral loading, controlled pressure loss, deflation, folding, and redeployment.

### Engineered-liquid testing

Fill, venting, density, viscosity, damping, leakage, thermal expansion, climate performance, drain, recovery, handling, and membrane compatibility.

### Inner containment barriers

Stored-content compatibility, permeability, sanitation, cleaning, wear, temperature, attachment, replacement, and secondary-containment behavior.

### Exterior protection

UV, weather, abrasion, cut, puncture, attachment, replacement, insulation, reflective surfaces, identity skins, and future defined-threat packages.

### Controlled damage and repair

Compartmentation, pressure and level detection, leak rate, isolation, patching, residual capacity, recovery, and return to service.

### Permanent and hardened development

Cementitious fill, cure, full-fill assurance, structural performance, anchoring, openings, third-party review, and defined-threat testing where applicable.

## Evidence status

Results will be published only at the level supported by the test. A material property does not automatically become a system rating, and a concept image does not become evidence.

# 7. ArchoOS

## Metadata

**Title:** ArchoOS | Configuration, Readiness and Lifecycle Evidence  
**Description:** ArchoOS is the planned control and evidence layer for configured ArchoSystem assets, covering identity, readiness, inspection, deployment, repair, replacement, and lifecycle history.

## Hero

**Eyebrow:** Control and evidence layer

# The physical asset needs a record as disciplined as the structure.

ArchoOS is the planned system for preserving what each asset is, how it was configured, where it is staged or deployed, what has been inspected, what changed, and whether it remains ready for service.

## Planned functions

### Configure

Preserve capacity, geometry, service class, inner barrier, climate package, exterior protection, accessories, and monitoring choices.

### Identify

Track serial numbers, component identity, material lots, replacement sections, accessories, sensors, and deployment kits.

### Inspect

Schedule and record storage inspections, readiness checks, deployment checks, repairs, calibration, recovery, refurbishment, and recertification.

### Monitor

Receive supported pressure, level, temperature, leak, location, and operating data as sensor integrations are developed.

### Record

Maintain deployment history, contents, location, repairs, replacement cycles, operator actions, evidence, and warranty information.

### Maintain readiness

Show staged inventory, missing components, expiring items, training status, replacement needs, and deployment readiness.

## Commercial role

ArchoTank creates the installed base. ArchoOS is intended to support per-asset, fleet, enterprise, and agency operating relationships around that base.

No live monitoring, compliance, or fleet-control capability is claimed until the relevant software and integrations are operational.
