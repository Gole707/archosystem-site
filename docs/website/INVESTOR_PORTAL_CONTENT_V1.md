# 1. Investment Overview

## Hero

**Eyebrow:** ArchoSystem investment overview

# A configurable containment platform built to create an installed base—not a one-time tank sale.

ArchoTank is the first market-entry product in a broader deployable-infrastructure platform. Each physical asset can create revenue from the initial system, application-specific barriers, structural and climate packages, exterior protection, accessories, aesthetic skins, ArchoOS, readiness, training, deployment, replacement, refurbishment, and fleet expansion.

## Executive thesis

### Physical entry product

ArchoTank enters through a large, established need: storing and containing liquids across emergency, municipal, industrial, agricultural, remote, defense, energy, construction, mining, and resilience applications.

### Configurable architecture

The product is not restricted to one transported tank size, one fixed structural medium, one content barrier, one climate, one duration, or one exterior package.

### Logistics advantage

The architecture is designed to separate transported package size from deployed capacity, creating a potential advantage wherever access, staging, response time, remote deployment, or large rigid components create friction.

### Installed-base engine

Every deployed asset can require accessories, replacement inventory, approved formulations, barriers, exterior packages, readiness inspection, training, recovery, refurbishment, recertification, and software records.

### Platform expansion

ArchoTank is the first physical product and validation engine for broader ArchoSystem infrastructure configurations.

## Investment question

The investor is not being asked to value a finished certified product. The investor is being asked to evaluate whether the architecture, market entry, validation plan, revenue stack, and capital-to-risk-removal path justify funding the next evidence milestones.

# 4. Product and Configuration Map

## Hero

# One architecture, configured across different operating jobs.

## Configuration classes

### Rapid deployable

Primary thesis: compact staging, faster field formation, lower heavy-equipment dependence, emergency and temporary capacity.

### Reusable staged capacity

Primary thesis: engineered structural or ballast media, climate packages, recovery, inspection, refurbishment, and redeployment.

### Semi-permanent

Primary thesis: longer-duration containment, anchoring, exterior protection, operating accessories, monitoring, and scheduled service.

### Permanent

Primary thesis: cementitious conversion to long-duration civil infrastructure while preserving controlled geometry and validation.

### Hardened

Primary thesis: future defined-threat configurations combining structural media, geometry, exterior protection, anchoring, openings, and system-level testing.

## Configuration axes

- capacity and geometry;
- inner containment barrier;
- structural or activation medium;
- climate formulation;
- exterior protection;
- identity and aesthetic package;
- base and restraint;
- access and process accessories;
- sensors and monitoring;
- readiness and lifecycle support.

## Product map output

Show which combinations serve which sectors, what is supported by precedent, what is calculated, what is planned, and what test or certification unlocks each market.

# 5. Revenue Engine

## Hero

# The tank creates the installed base. The system around it creates the relationship.

## Revenue layer 1 — Initial system

- structural containment body;
- content-specific inner barrier;
- exterior protection;
- approved activation or structural-medium package;
- base, anchoring, valves, connections, sensors, and deployment kit;
- installation and commissioning support.

## Revenue layer 2 — Staged readiness inventory

Target buyers may stage systems and replacement inventory before a disaster, outage, mission, planned shutdown, or seasonal need.

Potential commercial forms:

- direct purchase;
- reserve-capacity agreement;
- managed regional stockpile;
- lease;
- annual readiness contract;
- framework or indefinite-delivery vehicle;
- priority replenishment after deployment.

## Revenue layer 3 — Replacement materials and consumables

- climate and mission concentrates;
- replacement inner barriers;
- replacement exterior sections;
- repair kits;
- seals, gaskets, valves, fittings, batteries, sensors, and calibration items.

## Revenue layer 4 — Accessory fabrication

- ladders, stairs, platforms, handrails, and walkways;
- hatches and manways;
- vents, relief, overflow, drains, sampling, mixing, and recirculation;
- supports, brackets, equipment mounts, sensor rails, and restraints;
- roofs, insulation, heating, reflective surfaces, and weather packages;
- pumps, blowers, dosing skids, hose reels, controls, trailers, ground protection, and secondary containment.

## Revenue layer 5 — Aesthetic and identity skins

- logos and customer colors;
- municipal, agency, sponsor, donor, and humanitarian identity;
- emergency high-visibility;
- camouflage and environmental patterns;
- low-visibility, architectural, reflective, thermal, UV, safety, and fleet-identification packages.

These packages can provide margin, replacement revenue, and customer-specific differentiation without changing the structural core.

## Revenue layer 6 — ArchoOS

- per-asset subscription;
- fleet subscription;
- agency or enterprise license;
- readiness dashboard;
- inspection and recertification workflow;
- monitoring and alerts;
- repair and replacement scheduling;
- deployment history;
- compliance and evidence reporting;
- future API integration.

## Revenue layer 7 — Service and sustainment

- readiness inspection;
- training and certification;
- deployment exercises;
- emergency mobilization;
- recovery and decontamination;
- repair and component replacement;
- pressure testing and calibration;
- refurbishment, repacking, and recertification.

## Flywheel

> System sale → installed asset → accessories → ArchoOS → readiness contract → deployment → consumable and replacement use → refurbishment → redeployment → fleet expansion.

# 8. Validation and Risk-Removal Roadmap

## Hero

# Capital is useful only when it removes a defined risk.

## Milestone sequence

1. commercial-material and supplier shortlist;
2. certified material and fabrication data;
3. geometry, hydrostatic, stability, and structural calculations;
4. seam and fitting coupons;
5. full-height pneumatic prototype;
6. retained-liquid test;
7. engineered-liquid and climate tests;
8. inner-barrier compatibility;
9. exterior protection, cut, puncture, repair, and recovery;
10. repeated deployment and cycle testing;
11. third-party engineering review;
12. customer demonstration or pilot;
13. project-specific certification pathway;
14. permanent cementitious test;
15. defined-threat hardened test where commercially justified.

## Risk categories removed

- technical feasibility;
- material and supplier;
- fabrication repeatability;
- operating envelope;
- damage and repair;
- climate and compatibility;
- logistics and deployment;
- code and certification;
- customer adoption;
- unit economics;
- recurring-revenue behavior.
