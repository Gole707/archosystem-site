Motion and fallback
Native WebGL is the preferred renderer: at most 30 rendered frames/second, at most 1200px in either direction. If WebGL is unavailable, a bounded Canvas2D orthographic projection uses the same qualified local textures: at most 4 rendered frames/second, at most 480px in either direction. Only simple timing bookkeeping occurs between rendered frames. Slow rotation is illustrative (not the actual current orientation of Earth).

Pause control, reduced-motion default, offscreen/document-hidden suspension and route cleanup are implemented. If neither renderer can start, a static poster remains visible. On WebGL context loss, the static poster remains visible and the control reports Static Earth. Reinitializing the component or reopening the page can retry rendering.

Actual browser verification, 2026-10-29: this cloud browser reported WebGL unavailable. Canvas2D fallback rendered and rotated; the frame counter and longitude changed. Pause held frame 97 and angle -1.13160 stable across observations; Play resumed at frame 100. The renderer uses a 480x480 drawing surface in this desktop view. Scrolling the sphere fully offscreen held frame 26 and angle -1.30143 stable; returning to the hero resumed frame 27. WebGL output and context-loss behavior require a WebGL-capable browser and were not demonstrated here. Reduced-motion and hidden-document paths are implemented but were not independently emulated in this review.
