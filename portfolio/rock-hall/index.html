In construction · Rock Hall, MD

Rock Hall Wastewater Treatment Plant

Enhanced Nutrient Removal Upgrade

Rock Hall is upgrading its existing wastewater plant to remove more nitrogen and phosphorus before treated water reaches Gray’s Inn Creek and the Chesapeake Bay. Construction is being phased around the operating plant. [4] [6]

Why it happened

The Town is adding Enhanced Nutrient Removal to a plant that already serves Rock Hall and surrounding sewer customers. Reducing nutrient discharge matters downstream in Gray’s Inn Creek and the Chesapeake Bay. The Town assembled seven funding sources for the upgrade, including state and federal assistance. [1] [2] [4]

What’s being built

The project adds a post-anoxic reactor and denitrification filters to remove nitrogen. It also upgrades screening and grit removal, rehabilitates existing clarifiers, and improves disinfection and sludge handling. New process equipment, yard piping, and plant controls tie the upgraded systems into the existing facility. [5]

Construction progress

M2 shared photos of a concrete placement in April 2026. [7]

By October, work included excavation for the denitrification filters, rehabilitation of Clarifier No. 2, and foundation work for the dewatering building. The field work is sequenced around the treatment units the Town needs to keep in service.

What the upgrade is expected to change

Maryland’s Department of the Environment projects an 83% reduction in nitrogen pollution and a 70% reduction in phosphorus pollution once the upgraded plant is complete. [4]
